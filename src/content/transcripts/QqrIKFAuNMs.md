### Go to Have I Been Pwned (type it yourself)

Your email is probably sitting in a leaked database somewhere. Let's check — it takes 30 seconds.

Go to haveibeenpwned.com. Now, this is very important: type it yourself. Do not click it from
anywhere else, and especially don't click it from an email or an ad.

### Enter your email and read your results

Once you're there, enter your email and press Check. I'm going to use thisismyemail@example.com as
an example. Press Check — and surprisingly, there is one data breach on that email.

Once you can see how many data breaches you got, scroll down and you'll see each one of the
breaches: when it happened and exactly what was leaked. Do this for every email you've ever had.
Take a screenshot and keep it — you're going to need it.

### Two features I skip: the password check

Now, there are two features I skip. First is the password check. It's well built, but my rule is
simple: only type a password into the site it belongs to. Your password manager already flags
leaked ones anyway — something like 1Password, which you can even see from here. It can also be
Google or Apple.

### Why I skip Notify Me

The second one I won't use is the Notify Me button. Whether you got green or red, it doesn't
matter — I will not use Notify Me at all. Why? Because if I never sign up, then any breach alert
email that I get is fake. I can just come here and check manually every few months. It only takes
30 seconds.

### What to do if you got pwned

Now, if you did get pwned, change that password, plus anywhere else you reused it. Use a password
manager — again, like 1Password, Google, or Apple. Turn on two-factor authentication (or
multi-factor authentication), and start with your email. If something sensitive was leaked, like
your Social Security number, freeze your credit completely.

### What not to give a settlement site

Just very important: you do not have to pay anything to get your money back. And you shouldn't
have to input anything other than your email, because that's what was compromised — the lawyers
will probably just get back to you at some point. Other than that, do not provide PINs, passwords,
credit card data, and so on.

Also, it's probably going to be a few dollars, not a huge amount. So don't think it's going to be a
hundred thousand million dollars or something like that.
